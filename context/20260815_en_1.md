# A Brand-New Blog Visual Experience: Particle Engine, Frosted-Glass Navbar & the Immersive Reader

Over the past few months, this blog has been quietly growing: the Markdown rendering template, the TOC interactions, the comments Beta, and then the particle-background iteration of the homepage. This time, we turned our attention back to the reading experience itself — the very page you are looking at right now.

This article is an introduction. It walks you through the full story of this "brand-new visual experience" upgrade: how `pages/article.html` merges the **IP particle background engine**, a **floating frosted-glass navbar**, and **immersive typography** into a single reader page, along with the design intent and usage of each piece.

## Why Redesign the Reader

The article reader is the page with the highest content density and the longest dwell time on a blog. The old reader (`mobancopy.html`) reused the site-wide fixed navbar and plain white background. It was functionally complete, but it lacked a "reading atmosphere": no reading progress, no ambient background, and the reading experience looked almost identical to the list pages.

The goals of this upgrade were clear:

- ✅ **Build a reading atmosphere**: particle background + ambient glow + reading progress bar, so long-form reading no longer feels dull
- ✅ **Unify the visual language**: stay consistent with the frosted-glass navbar and segmented day/night switch of the homepage `index_new.html`
- ✅ **Sharpen reading focus**: serif body text, Hero-style titles, and a lightweight TOC that reduce visual noise
- ✅ **Do not break the existing ecosystem**: all new styles are scoped, so legacy pages and scripts are unaffected

## Visual Theme: Two "Skins" for Day and Night

The new reader no longer relies on scattered color patches. Instead, it manages the theme with a complete CSS variable system. Day mode uses a warm cream background (`#f4eee4`) with muted blue-gray accents; night mode switches to deep-sea blue (`#07172b`) with soft cool-white text. Both skins share the same variable names, and switching only changes the `light-mode / dark-mode` class on `body`.

| Variable | Day | Night | Purpose |
|----------|-----|-------|---------|
| `--mdx-bg` | `#f4eee4` | `#07172b` | Page background |
| `--mdx-text` | `#202832` | `#edf3fa` | Primary text |
| `--mdx-text-soft` | `#68717b` | `#aab8c8` | Secondary body text |
| `--mdx-accent` | `#5f88a7` | `#8bbbe2` | Accent color (links, indicator bars) |
| `--mdx-glass` | `rgba(255,255,255,.58)` | `rgba(15,34,57,.72)` | Frosted-glass card background |
| `--mdx-border` | `rgba(70,78,86,.10)` | `rgba(190,220,245,.10)` | Borders |

```css
:root {
    --mdx-bg: #f4eee4;
    --mdx-text: #202832;
    --mdx-accent: #5f88a7;
    --mdx-glass: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.58);
}

body.dark-mode {
    --mdx-bg: #07172b;
    --mdx-text: #edf3fa;
    --mdx-accent: #8bbbe2;
    --mdx-glass: rgba(15, 34, 57, 0.72);
}
```

The interesting part: this variable set also remaps the legacy component colors (`--primary-color`, `--text-color`, etc.), so inherited components such as the TOC, the comments section, and the print/share buttons automatically follow the new skins — no per-component patching needed.

## The IP Particle Background: An Avatar Easter Egg in the Corner

If the new theme is the "skin", the particle background is the "soul". This part comes from the **IP particle engine** in `pages/demo/newbottombackgroundbate.html`: it samples the avatar image `7903.jpg` pixel by pixel into hundreds of particles, each carrying its own color, which slowly assemble into a "particle portrait" in the lower-right corner of the page.

- **A subtle easter egg**: the IP is capped at 430px wide and particle opacity is deliberately low (from 0.18), following the principle of "you notice it only after you discover it" — it never competes with the text
- **Mouse magnetic field**: nearby particles get a soft repulsion plus tangential swirl (repulsion, not attraction, to avoid particle pile-up), with a fluid drag trail as the cursor moves
- **Click pulse**: pressing the mouse fires a one-shot burst that briefly scatters nearby particles before they drift back
- **Day/night transition**: particles keep their original colors in day mode and blend toward blue-white at night; `themeProgress` interpolates continuously with a 0.035 damping factor, so theme switching is a smooth "gradient" rather than a hard "jump"

Performance is deliberately restrained as well: DPR capped at 1.5 (protecting high-DPI devices), spatial-grid collision to avoid O(n²) connections, sampling step raised from 6 to 9 automatically on mobile, and particle caps of 850 (mobile) / 1500 (desktop).

```javascript
// Change only this line to swap in your own IP image
const MDX_IP_IMAGE_SRC = "./7903.jpg";

// The max size of the IP: it is a background easter egg, not a giant illustration
const MDX_IP_MAX_WIDTH = 430;
```

## The Floating Frosted-Glass Navbar

The navbar went from "fixed at the top, edge to edge" to a **floating frosted-glass capsule centered at the top**: 26px from the top, 20px radius, `backdrop-filter: blur(22px) saturate(130%)`, so the content softly blurs beneath the glass as you scroll — the depth appears instantly.

The navbar uses a three-part layout:

1. **Brand**: avatar + site name, linking back to the homepage
2. **Navigation**: Home / Blog / Links / Resume / About, with the current page highlighted
3. **Actions**: GitHub and Email icon buttons + the segmented day/night switch + the 中/EN language slider

### Day/Night Switch: Fully Unified with the Site

The new segmented switch (☀ Day / ☾ Night) is not a toy confined to this page — it drives the site-wide theme convention directly:

```javascript
document.body.classList.remove("light-mode", "dark-mode");
document.body.classList.add(nextDark ? "dark-mode" : "light-mode");
localStorage.setItem("theme", nextDark ? "dark-mode" : "light-mode");
```

In other words, switch to night mode in the reader, and the homepage and blog list are still in night mode — the `localStorage` preference is shared. At the same time, the particle background and code highlighting (atom-one-light / dark) switch in sync. A hidden legacy `theme-toggle` placeholder is kept in the page so old scripts like `js/theme.js` remain compatible.

### Language Switch: Reusing the Site-Wide Bilingual Mechanism

The language slider also reuses the `data-en / data-zh` system from `language.js`: click EN, the slider glides right, every bilingual label on the page updates at once, and a `sitewide-language-change` event is broadcast — the reader listens for it and automatically reloads the paired language file via the `_zh.md ↔ _en.md` convention.

> A small detail: language switching only changes which Markdown file is loaded; the query string in the address bar stays untouched, so shared links remain language-neutral.

## Reading Progress Bar & Immersive Typography

A 2px gradient progress bar sits at the top of the page and advances smoothly with scrolling, so readers always know "how much of this article is left". It is a very light visual cue — unobtrusive, yet it greatly improves the sense of control over long articles.

The body typography is the quietest yet most carefully crafted part of this upgrade:

- **Hero-style title**: serif font (Georgia / Noto Serif SC), `clamp(34px, 4.2vw, 54px)` fluid sizing, negative letter-spacing for a tighter, more powerful headline
- **Serif body text**: 2.0 line height, so long Chinese passages read more comfortably; `strong` emphasis uses the primary text color — emphasized without being harsh
- **Rounded code cards**: code blocks become soft 18px-radius cards with a deep blue-black background in dark mode
- **Frosted-glass comments**: the comments section is condensed into a frosted-glass card, keeping draft autosave and the submit cooldown
- **Fine details**: dividers are 80px gradient dashes; blockquotes get a 3px accent left border; images get a uniform 20px radius

For math, this round also fixed a misjudgment: price/amount ranges in body text (e.g. $1,900→$2,040) used to be mistaken for inline formulas, producing wide, non-wrapping KaTeX fragments. The renderer now guards against this — phrases containing Chinese characters, Markdown syntax, dashes/arrows, or natural-language text without any LaTeX command are left as-is:

```javascript
if (/[\u4e00-\u9fa5]|\]\(|!\[|\*\*|→|–|—/.test(formula)) {
    return match; // keep the original text, do not treat as a formula
}
```

## TOC & Reading Tools

The right-hand TOC is a Notion-style lightweight panel: no borders, local scrolling with a hidden scrollbar, subtle hover scaling, and a 3px accent indicator bar on the right of the active item. While you scroll, the TOC highlights the current section in real time and automatically keeps the active item visible. The collapse buttons support expanding/collapsing third-level headings.

The article header keeps four reading tools: **Print, Share, Fullscreen, and Font Size**. Font size cycles through "Normal / Large / Extra Large / Small", and the preference is stored in `localStorage` and restored the next time you open the same page.

## Documentation & Usage Guide

If you are the site owner or a collaborator, here is how to use this reader:

### Loading a Markdown Article

Specify the file path via the `md` or `file` query parameter:

```
pages/article.html?md=../../context/20260815_en_1.md
```

The page automatically resolves the `_zh.md ↔ _en.md` pairing according to the current language: if the URL points to `20260815_zh_1.md`, switching to English tries `20260815_en_1.md`; numbered series (`_zh_1`, `_zh_2`) pair the same way.

### Configuring the Default Article & Metadata

Without parameters, the page loads the default file and default metadata from `PAGE_CONFIG`:

```javascript
const PAGE_CONFIG = {
    defaultMarkdownFile: '../../context/test_zh.md',
    articleMeta: {
        title: 'Markdown测试文章',
        title_en: 'Markdown Test Article',
        date: '2026-08-15',
        category: '技术',
        category_en: 'Technology',
        tags: ['Markdown', '博客', '视觉体验'],
        tags_en: ['Markdown', 'Blog', 'Visual Experience']
    }
};
```

The article title comes from the first Markdown line (`# Title`); after rendering it is removed from the body to avoid duplication. Date, category, and tags are read from the metadata registered in `blog-posts-data.js` when available.

### Customizing the IP Image

Place your avatar image in `pages/blog/` and change `MDX_IP_IMAGE_SRC` (a square or near-square image works best for particle sampling).

## Compatibility & Design Principles

Finally, the most important constraint of this upgrade: **do not break anything old**.

- All new styles are scoped under `mdx-*` and `.md-article-layout`, so the legacy article pages, homepage, and blog list are completely unaffected
- The old template `mobancopy.html` is kept as a backup for easy comparison and rollback
- Three responsive breakpoints (1250 / 900 / 580px): narrow screens hide the text nav and language slider while keeping the mobile experience intact
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is supported — animations are drastically shortened when the OS requests reduced motion
- Script validation: every inline script in this update passed `node --check`

## Closing Thoughts

From today on, the blog's article page is no longer a default "white background + body text" template, but a warm, layered reading space: particles breathing in the corner, a glass navbar floating above, a progress bar silently tracking your reading journey, and day/night and Chinese/English switching at the touch of a button.

The technology itself is not complicated — a layer of CSS variables, a chunk of canvas sampling, a few event listeners — but when they come together, reading becomes a more enjoyable experience. I hope you enjoy this brand-new blog reader, and feel free to leave your feedback in the comments.
